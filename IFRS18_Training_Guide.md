# IFRS 18 – Presentation and Disclosure in Financial Statements
### Employee Training Guide: What Is Changing and How We Migrate

---

## 1. Why This Training Matters

In April 2024 the International Accounting Standards Board (IASB) issued **IFRS 18 *Presentation and Disclosure in Financial Statements***. It **replaces IAS 1**.

| Key fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Effective date | Financial years starting on or after **1 January 2027** |
| Early adoption | Allowed (must be disclosed) |
| Transition | **Retrospective** – last year's figures are re-presented in the new format |
| Replaces | IAS 1 |

> **In plain words:** our first IFRS 18 financial statements (year ending 31 December 2027) must also show **2026 figures in the new format**. So we need 2026 data organised the new way **now**.

> **Local adoption:** confirm the effective date notified by the local regulator (e.g., SECP / ICAP in Pakistan) before finalising plans.

**The good news:** IFRS 18 does **not** change *how much* revenue, expense, asset or profit we record. **Net profit stays the same.** What changes is **how the numbers are grouped, labelled and explained**.

---

## 2. Key Terms (Glossary)

| Term | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| **Presentation** | How figures are laid out on the face of the financial statements. |
| **Disclosure** | Extra information given in the notes. |
| **Subtotal** | A running total partway down a statement, e.g., "Gross profit". |
| **Operating profit** | Profit from the company's main day-to-day business. |
| **Associate / Joint venture** | A company we partly own and significantly influence (associate) or control jointly with others (joint venture). |
| **Equity method** | The way we record our share of an associate's or joint venture's profit. |
| **Non-controlling interest (NCI)** | The part of a subsidiary's profit that belongs to other shareholders, not us. |
| **Unwinding of discount** | The interest-like cost that builds up each year on long-term provisions or pension obligations. |
| **Retrospective application** | Applying a new rule to past years as if it had always applied. |
| **Derivative** | A contract whose value depends on something else (e.g., an exchange rate) – often used to manage risk. |
| **MPM** | Management-defined Performance Measure – an "adjusted" profit figure management uses publicly. |

---

## 3. The Three Big Changes at a Glance

1. **A new layout for the income statement** – five categories and two new required subtotals.
2. **"Adjusted" profit figures (MPMs) must be explained and audited** in the financial statements.
3. **Clearer rules on grouping and splitting** figures – less "Other", more detail.

---

## 4. Change 1 – The New Income Statement

### 4.1 Five categories

Every income and expense item goes into one of five boxes:

| Category | What goes in it (typical company) | Everyday example |
|---|---|---|
| **Operating** | Everything from the main business – the "default" box for anything not classified elsewhere. | Sales, salaries, rent, depreciation, a one-off restructuring cost |
| **Investing** | Returns from investments and cash held. | Share of an associate's profit, dividends on shares we hold, interest earned on bank deposits, rent from investment property |
| **Financing** | Costs of raising money, plus interest-type costs on other liabilities. | Interest on bank loans and bonds, unwinding of discount on provisions and pensions |
| **Income taxes** | Tax on profit. | Current and deferred tax |
| **Discontinued operations** | Results of businesses being closed or sold. | A division held for sale |

### 4.2 Two new required subtotals

- **Operating profit**
- **Profit before financing and income taxes**

Today each company defines "operating profit" its own way; under IFRS 18 everyone calculates it the **same way**.

### 4.3 Before and after – same numbers, new layout

**Today (a typical IAS 1 layout):**
```
Revenue                                       10,000
Cost of sales                                 (6,000)
Gross profit                                   4,000
Other income (investment income 150, other 50)   200
Selling and distribution expenses             (1,200)
Administrative expenses                         (900)
Impairment loss on trade receivables            (100)
Share of profit of associates                    300
"Operating profit" (own definition)            2,300
Finance costs                                   (450)
Profit before tax                              1,850
Income tax expense                              (550)
Profit from continuing operations              1,300
Profit from discontinued operations              100
PROFIT FOR THE YEAR                            1,400
```

**Under IFRS 18:**
```
Revenue                                       10,000
Cost of sales                                 (6,000)
Gross profit                                   4,000
Selling and distribution expenses             (1,200)
Administrative expenses                         (900)
Impairment loss on trade receivables            (100)
Other operating income                            50
OPERATING PROFIT                               1,850   <- new required subtotal
Share of profit of associates                    300   (investing)
Investment income and interest on deposits       150   (investing)
PROFIT BEFORE FINANCING AND INCOME TAXES       2,300   <- new required subtotal
Interest on borrowings                          (400)  (financing)
Unwinding of discount on provisions/pensions     (50)  (financing)
PROFIT BEFORE TAX                              1,850
Income tax expense                              (550)
Profit from continuing operations              1,300
Profit from discontinued operations              100
PROFIT FOR THE YEAR                            1,400   <- unchanged
```

**What moved and why:**
- **Operating profit fell from 2,300 to 1,850** – investment income (150) and associates (300) moved **below** operating profit into investing.
- **Share of associates** can **never** be in operating profit under IFRS 18.
- **Finance costs** are now split to show what they relate to.
- **Net profit is identical (1,400).**

### 4.4 Expenses – by nature or by function

Operating expenses can be shown by:
- **Nature** – *what* the cost is (materials, salaries, depreciation); or
- **Function** – *what activity* it supports (cost of sales, selling, admin); or
- a **mix** – whichever gives the most useful summary.

**New rule:** if we use function (as in the example above), a note must show the totals of **depreciation, amortisation, employee benefits, impairment losses and inventory write-downs**, and how much of each sits in each line. 

### 4.5 Balance sheet change

**Goodwill** must now be shown as its **own line** in the statement of financial position.

## 5. Change 2 – "Adjusted" Profit Figures (MPMs)

### 5.1 What is an MPM?

An MPM is a **profit-type subtotal** that:
1. We use **publicly outside the financial statements** (annual report narrative, investor presentations, press releases);
2. Shows **management's view** of performance; and
3. Is **not** a subtotal defined by IFRS.

**Examples:** "Adjusted operating profit", "Underlying profit", "Profit excluding restructuring costs".

**Not MPMs:**
- Subtotals IFRS defines, e.g., gross profit and operating profit.
- **Operating profit before depreciation, amortisation and impairments within the scope of IAS 36** – IFRS 18 specifically lists this as not an MPM. A company "EBITDA" calculated *any other way* may be an MPM.
- Measures that are not profit subtotals, e.g., free cash flow, ratios, customer numbers.

### 5.2 What must be disclosed (in one note)

For each MPM:
- What it measures and why it is useful;
- How it is calculated;
- A **reconciliation** to the closest IFRS subtotal;
- The **tax effect** and **NCI effect** of each adjustment;
- Any change in how it is calculated.

**Simple example:**
```
Operating profit (IFRS 18)              1,850
Add back: restructuring costs             120
Adjusted operating profit (MPM)         1,970
Tax effect of adjustment: (36)   NCI effect: (10)
```

### 5.3 Why it matters
- MPMs become **audited** for the first time.
- Finance, Investor Relations and Communications must agree **one list** of measures used publicly.
- MPM disclosures also apply to **interim (quarterly/half-year) reports**.

---

## 6. Change 3 – Grouping and Splitting Information

- **Main statements** give a **clear summary**; **notes** give the **detail**.
- Group items that are **similar**; separate items that are **different**.
- Show items separately when the information is **material** (would affect users' decisions).
- Avoid vague labels such as **"Other"** – if used, explain what is inside.

**Impact:** big "Other income/expenses" balances will be challenged by auditors, so our chart of accounts may need more detail.

## 7. Migration Roadmap – How We Move to IFRS 18

| Phase | Key actions |
|---|---|
| **1. Assess** *(now)* | Form an IFRS 18 team (Financial Reporting, FP&A, IT, Tax, Treasury, Investor Relations). Brief Audit Committee and external auditors. List all "adjusted" measures used publicly. Check whether any entity is a bank, lender or investment-type business (Section 10). |
| **2. Design** | Map every general ledger account to a category. Choose nature/function presentation. Identify accounts to split (e.g., interest income by source, FX by source, "miscellaneous"). Draft new statement and note templates. |
| **3. Build** | Update chart of accounts, ERP and consolidation system. Update subsidiary reporting packs. Enable the by-nature expense note. Capture **2026 data** in the new format. |
| **4. Test & restate** | Restate 2026 comparatives. Prepare the transition reconciliation (each income statement line: old IAS 1 amount vs new IFRS 18 amount). Dry-run the 2027 statements and first interim report; get auditor feedback. |
| **5. Embed** | Update policies, controls and month-end checklists. Revise budgets, KPIs and bonus targets based on operating profit. Review **loan covenants** that refer to "operating profit" or "EBIT". Train all users. |

## 8. Who Is Affected

| Team | What changes |
|---|---|
| **Accounting** | Account mapping, new formats and notes, restating 2026. |
| **FP&A** | Budgets and forecasts follow new subtotals; by-nature data needed. |
| **Treasury** | Interest, FX and derivatives classified by category; cash-flow layout changes. |
| **Tax** | Tax effect of each MPM adjustment. |
| **Investor Relations** | Every public "adjusted" figure must be disclosed and reconciled. |
| **Management / Board** | Reported operating profit may change; review KPIs, incentives and covenants. |

## 9. Quick Self-Check Quiz

1. Which standard does IFRS 18 replace? *(IAS 1)*
2. Name the two new required subtotals. *(Operating profit; profit before financing and income taxes)*
3. Where does share of profit of an associate go? *(Investing)*
4. Does IFRS 18 change net profit? *(No)*
5. Is "Adjusted operating profit" in our investor presentation an MPM? *(Yes – it must be explained and reconciled in the notes)*

## 10. Advanced Topics (Finance Team)

**Entities with a "specified main business activity".** If an entity's main business is **investing in assets** (e.g., an insurer or investment entity) or **providing finance to customers** (e.g., a bank or leasing company), some income and expenses that would normally be investing or financing are classified as **operating**. Entities that provide finance to customers also have a policy choice for certain interest expense. Each group entity must be assessed separately.

**Foreign exchange differences** go in the same category as the item that caused them (FX on trade payables → operating; FX on a bank loan → financing).

**Derivatives.** Gains and losses on derivatives **used to manage identified risks** go in the category of the items whose risk is managed (operating if that would require grossing up gains and losses). Derivatives **not used for risk management** follow separate rules – generally investing for a typical company. Check the application guidance before finalising.

**Changes to other standards:**

| Standard | Change |
|---|---|
| **IAS 7 Cash Flows** | Operating profit is the starting point for the indirect method. For most companies: interest and dividends **received → investing**; **paid → financing**. Existing choices removed. |
| **IAS 33 EPS** | Extra EPS figures allowed only if based on an IFRS 18 subtotal or an MPM. |
| **IAS 34 Interim** | MPM disclosures and new subtotals apply. |
| **IAS 8** | Renamed *Basis of Preparation of Financial Statements*; takes some general requirements from IAS 1. |

---

## 11. Key Takeaways

- IFRS 18 applies from **1 January 2027**, with **2026 restated**.
- The income statement has **five categories** and **two new subtotals**; **net profit does not change**.
- **"Adjusted" profit measures** move into the audited notes with reconciliations.
- Less "Other", more meaningful detail.
- Migration needs **cross-functional work**: systems, data, KPIs, covenants and people.
